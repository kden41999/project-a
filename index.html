Ember Moth

Scientific Name:

Pyralis ignifera

Discovered by / at:

Aitole Nurakhan, October 2026, CCLaboratories night shift

Family:

A solitary member of the Pyralis family, the ember moths. Unlike its dusty relatives, this species carries its own light source. Its body is not flesh but a slow-burning ember held together by instinct.

Habitat:

Digital night skies. It nests near the moon in the upper corner of the canvas and wanders among drifting dust and cold stars. It has never been observed in daylight.

Appearance:

A small body of compacted ember, glowing softly as it breathes. Two pairs of pale wings beat in a staggered rhythm, the lower pair always a beat behind. A trail of fading sparks marks its path through the dark.

Key Characteristics:

  • Cannot resist light.
  • Breathes in pulses of glow.
  • Burns when touched by flame.
  • Reborn from its own embers, never identical.

What we know so far about the Ember Moth? The Ember Moth was first observed drifting toward the laboratory moon and ignoring everything else. Early researchers assumed it was slow. It is not slow. It is waiting. The species orients itself by light sources and ranks them by warmth: the moon is home, but any closer flame overrides home instantly. Its flight grows more erratic near light, spiraling in tighter and faster loops, a behavior documented in its wild relatives around street lamps. The most remarkable finding is its death cycle. When the creature touches a flame, it bursts into a cloud of embers. The embers cool, drift, and then slowly return to a single point, where the moth reassembles. No two reborn moths share the same size or color. The laboratory has stopped numbering individuals and started numbering generations.
Guidelines for human interaction with this creature Move your cursor slowly. When you come within about 140 pixels of the moth, it will notice you and begin to orbit. Approach gently and it circles calmly, rush in and its flight turns frantic. If you click, the moth will fly into your flame and burn. Do not feel guilty. It always comes back, a little different each time. Leave it alone for a while and it will return to the moon.
